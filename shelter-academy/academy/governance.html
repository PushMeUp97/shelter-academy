Domain 1: IT Governance Architecture

Establishing an effective corporate structure to ensure security strategies directly enable business outcomes.

Organizational Alignments & Frameworks

IT Governance ensures enterprise goals are achieved by evaluating stakeholder needs, setting strategic direction, and monitoring compliance against agreed objectives. Organizations typically rely on industry standards such as COBIT, ISO/IEC 27001, and ITIL to build their internal governance strategies.

Key Principle: Governance is not just an IT concern. It requires explicit accountability from executive management and the Board of Directors to define risk appetite and establish internal controls.

Enterprise Security Policies

Policies serve as the foundation of an enterprise's information security program. They establish mandatory high-level requirements that define user expectations, access patterns, and organizational posture:

  • Acceptable Use Policy (AUP): Outlines formal constraints regarding computer and network utilization within company parameters.
  • Access Control Policy: Dictates logical permission baselines based on roles, following the principle of least privilege.
  • Data Classification Standard: Identifies asset tiers (Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted) to allocate defensive spending efficiently.
