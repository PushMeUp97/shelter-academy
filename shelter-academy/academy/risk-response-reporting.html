Domain 3: Risk Response Implementation & Reporting

Executing risk remediation plans and designing key performance indicators for leadership review.

The Four Strategic Risk Options

Once a risk is formally evaluated, management must select one of four standard treatment strategies:

  • Mitigation (Reduction): Deploying safeguards or administrative controls to reduce risk to an acceptable level (e.g., implementing an encrypted SMTP relay configuration or hardening web nodes).
  • Transference (Sharing): Shifting the financial burden of the risk to an outside entity, typically via third-party vendors or cybersecurity insurance.
  • Avoidance: Eliminating the risk entirely by stopping the underlying activity or decommissioning the high-risk technical asset.
  • Acceptance: Formally acknowledging the baseline risk without deploying additional safeguards, typically because the remediation costs outweigh the asset value.

Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) & Dashboard Metrics

KRIs act as early-warning operational metrics that track changes in an organization's risk profile over time. Unlike KPIs (which measure historical performance), KRIs look forward to predict potential breaches or policy exceptions before they happen.

Formulating Risk Exceptions

When operational needs conflict with established corporate policies, teams should never simply bypass controls. Instead, they must submit a formal Risk Exception Request. This process requires explicitly documenting the business justification, listing compensating controls to minimize danger, setting a strict expiration date, and obtaining formal signature approval from an authorized manager.
